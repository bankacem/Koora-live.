Chelsea's Two Right-Backs in Midfield After £349m Spending: Is Squad Depth Still an Issue?

9/6/2026 · By Koora Live

BBC Sport reports that Chelsea’s 2-1 loss to AC Monaco–the champions of Ligue 1–left the Blues’ manager with an unconventional set‑up on the field. With both right‑backs positioned beside each other in the middle of the pitch, the match highlighted a strategic gamble that raises questions about the squad’s balance, despite the club’s record transfer outlay of £349 million in the summer. The decision prompts a closer look at how Chelsea’s tactical planning, squad depth, and transfer spending converge to affect performance in a single‑match high‑stakes environment such as the Premier League. This piece will unpack the reasoning behind the two‑RB midfield and consider whether the club’s recent financial commitment truly translated into on‑field depth.

Tactical Shift: Why Two Right‑Backs in the Middle?

The choice to deploy two right‑backs in a midfield partnership is a relatively rare move in Premier League football, more commonly associated with teams that prioritize width and overloads in wide channels. By pushing the right‑backs forward, Chelsea aimed to create dynamic overloads on the opposition’s left flank while simultaneously ensuring a solid defensive shape when the ball was lost.

Historically, clubs such as Barcelona and France’s national team have employed full‑back forays in midfield to maintain possession and generate off‑sides traps, but doing so in a defensive‑heavy league poses significant risks. Analysts might see this as a sign that manager Graham Potter was experimenting with roles to keep the squad flexible in the face of fluctuating injury counts.

The scheme also reflects a broader trend in modern tactics where full‑backs can double as versatile midfielders, capable of tracking back or joining attacks. For Chelsea, the short‑term decision to place the right‑backs in the centre possibly signals a desire to fix a perceived gap in creative they felt was not adequately addressed by the existing midfield options.

Transfer Spending and Squad Depth: £349m Revisited

The summer’s £349 million outlay, the highest it has ever spent, was concentrated on acquiring high‑profile talent across all positions, with a particular emphasis on the attacking centre through Eden Hughes and a solid defensive core via Christian Pulisic and others. Despite the financial heft, the question remains whether the acquisition strategy yielded a truly balanced squad.

Historically, Chelsea’s ownership has employed a strategy of buying and loaning a large number of players, which can create a surplus tier of talent but may also hinder clear integration. The modern squad at Stamford Bridge contains a mix of experienced stars and young prospects, and managing this group requires regular rotation.

The decision to push two right‑backs into midfield may thus be a symptom of a deeper issue: the limited competitive match time for newly signed or young players. In the context of a demanding Premier League calendar paired with continental competitions, rotating players appropriately becomes a tightrope walk between maintaining form and providing development.

Analysis

While the manager’s tactical deployment may be viewed as a creative risk, it also underscores a potential lack of quality depth across key midfield positions. The club’s sizeable investment is undeniable, yet the reliance on experienced players like Pierre‑Emerick Hasting and the older core implies a shortage of middle‑field dynamism that a single game can expose. Anomalies such as this could indicate that depth analysis and budget allocation might need recalibration, ensuring that high-spending does not simply translate into a select group of players with limited alternative roles.

Chelsea’s experiment of two right‑backs in the midfield spotlights the tension between the club’s hefty transfer budget and on‑field versatility. Whether the move signals a genuine tactical evolution or highlights a shortfall in squad depth remains a talking point for fans and analysts alike. Time will tell if this approach becomes a new chapter in the club’s evolving playstyle.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why did Chelsea use two right‑backs in midfield during the match?

According to the analysis, the strategy was to create width on the right side while maintaining a solid defensive base when needed. This approach, while uncommon, is sometimes employed to overload a particular area of the pitch, offering better defensive cover in case of ball loss.

Does £349m in transfer spending guarantee a deep squad?

High spending can provide access to a large pool of talent, but without a clear rotation and integration strategy, depth may remain theoretical. Chelsea’s current squad suggests that while several quality players are on the roster, competition for playing time can create bottlenecks.

Is the two‑right‑back tactic common in Premier League clubs?

It is relatively rare for Premier League teams to consistently place two full‑backs in midfield. The tactic is more typically seen in occasional match experiments rather than as a standard tactical approach.
